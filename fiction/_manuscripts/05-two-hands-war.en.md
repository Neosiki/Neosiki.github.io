## I. Bombardment

The night on Maldek was not dark.

The planet had been under bombardment for so long that its atmosphere held the light. Each time the Martian fleet's photon cannons swept the edge of a continent, the sky bled red. Light spread through the clouds, and the sea boiled black.

The fleet descended slowly from beyond orbit. There was no reason to hurry. Maldek had no weapon that could reach them there.

A photon beam makes no sound. Sound comes only after it arrives. Twelve atmospheric purification towers along the northern coast went dark at once, and three seconds later the sound rolled inland. People saw the towers fail before they heard them fail. Those three seconds were the whole of this war.

Thirteen dome cities burned. Underground waterways collapsed, and water rose from the floors of the cities. The transparent shells over them had been designed to withstand heat. They had not been designed to withstand water.

Six defense satellites circled the planet at a slow drift. The Rene had built them. Now the satellites had turned against the fleet that made them. Their resonance was close to breaking.

Maldek had once been a world shared by two peoples. The Chiwoo chose the place, and the Rene built upon it. That order had held for a long time. No one could say exactly when it stopped holding.

## II. The Ring of Stones

Ara sat inside the ring of stones at the observatory.

There were seven stones. None had been shaped with precision. One was cracked. Another lay half buried in sand. The Rene would not have called it a structure. Neither would the Chiwoo.

This was a place.

The Chiwoo knew how to build. They had chosen not to. What is built remains, and what remains fixes a direction. The Chiwoo could not bear for a direction to be fixed.

Ara closed her eyes.

From across the planet, the thoughts of the Chiwoo flowed into her.

The depth calculated one last time by someone trapped in water. A technician who had not yet let go beneath a collapsing tower. The brief instant in which someone chose which way to push a child. People who knew it was already too late and chose to believe it was not.

Ara bound them all into a single bundle. Maldek's magnetic field and hundreds of thousands of minds joined into one ring.

The defense lattice came alive.

It was not a wall. A wall cannot stop light. What Ara made was a structure that split the paths by which light could arrive. It did not prevent arrival. It created too many places where arrival might occur.

The next barrage came.

Six beams divided the instant they touched the upper atmosphere. One vanished there. Another fell into an uninhabited desert in the southern hemisphere and left a basin of glass. Twelve afterimages scattered into space. None reached a city.

In orbit, every targeting screen in the Martian fleet went blank at once.

## III. The Four Gates

Las was in the flagship's alignment chamber.

There was no target on the screen. The Chiwoo defense lattice had covered the planet in darkness. Optics found nothing.

Las switched target identification from light to mass. What cannot be seen still has weight. A planet cannot hide its own weight.

The outline of Maldek returned to the screen. This time it was drawn not in light but in gravity. The terrain was gone. Only density remained, a planet suspended on the display in gray contour lines.

> **Martian War Council:** “Is this Chiwoo psychic interference?”

> **Las:** “No. They have divided the paths of arrival. If we continue firing, the beams will continue to divide.”

> **Martian War Council:** “Then fire something that cannot be divided.”

Four satellites began to shift position in orbit.

Adenas. Magnes. Armos. Porus.

They had once been the defenses of Mars. They had been built to protect it. Now the center of each satellite opened. Concentric rings drew back one by one, revealing a long, dark aperture. No light came from within. A planetary-core penetrator does not shine. It is simply open.

The Rene specification lines etched into the satellites glowed red. The lines on all four were identical. They had come from the same plan.

Las had once drawn that standard with his own hands. It was when he built gates.

The technology that raises a civilization is the same technology that can break a planet. The Rene had known this for a long time. They knew it and refused to give the two things different names, because the moment they did, they would have to abandon one of them.

The neural shield at the base of Las's neck trembled faintly. Every Rene soldier carried one. It did not erase emotion. It compressed emotion into a single point and drove it outward. What had been moved rather than erased continued to gather density somewhere else.

Inside the shield, Las carried one memory.

A gate began to open, then closed. Of its three alignment layers, only the outer one had activated. The two inner layers did not follow. His sister had been inside. The coordinates were exact. Everything matched the plan. It was exact, and still she did not arrive.

From that day on, Las became a man who erased error from every value. He believed the gate would not have closed if there had been no error.

## IV. Interlock

That was when Ara sensed Las.

Among tens of thousands of thoughts, one registered differently. A shielded mind is not quiet. Compression gives it density. Ara found that density.

> **Ara:** “I see where you will fire before you fire.”

An instrument in Las's alignment chamber faltered for a beat. He had not heard a voice. It was the sensation that something already knew the result of his calculation before he did.

The Rene still possessed a trace of attunement. It had dwindled from disuse until it could no longer be trusted. With that diminished sense, Las recognized Ara.

> **Las:** “Without my calculations, you cannot arrive at the place you see.”

They were looking at the same battlefield. One saw it as the direction of possibility. The other saw it as coordinates.

The battle quickened.

Ara shifted the density of the defense lattice toward the western continent. Las corrected his targeting eastward. Ara moved it again. This time Las anticipated her direction and locked onto two coordinates at once. Ara placed the lattice's density at neither point, nor anywhere between them. The barrage arrived in empty space.

The four satellites corrected their positions second by second. In a long exposure, their orbits would have remained as four thin white lines. The rest of the fleet could not keep pace. The chain of command was slower than the two of them.

And Ara knew she could not win at this speed.

The lattice could only delay arrival. Once the four penetrators completed alignment, arrival could no longer be divided. The order to fire something that could not be divided had been precise.

## V. 0.4 Degrees

Ara widened the ring until it encircled the planet.

Hundreds of thousands of minds aligned in a single direction. She took hold of Maldek's magnetic field, through the field the distribution of mass beneath the crust, and through that mass the planet itself.

Only then could the Chiwoo touch a planet. A people who had chosen not to build could do one thing in place of building: move what was already there, just a little.

Ara twisted.

**0.4 degrees.**

The planet's rotational axis moved by 0.4 degrees. To the human eye, nothing had happened.

The sea knew first.

The currents changed direction. The northern ocean shifted out of place and tilted toward the continent. The water did not pass over the surface. It went beneath it. It entered the dome cities through the broken waterways.

Tens of thousands of Chiwoo were trapped under water.

Ara received every sensation. Joined through the ring, she could miss none of them. This was the result of the direction she had changed.

In orbit, Las's instrument produced a value.

> **Las:** “The 0.4 degrees you changed are killing the cities.”

> **Ara:** “I know.”

> **Las:** “You can stop.”

> **Ara:** “If I stop, your penetrators complete alignment.”

Las looked at the screen.

Maldek's rotational axis and magnetic axis no longer coincided. The penetrators used the rotational axis as their targeting reference. The true center of the core no longer lay upon it.

0.4 degrees.

Erase that value, and the penetrators would strike the exact center of the core.

## VI. Alignment

Las connected to the control signals of all four satellites.

He tried to change the alignment values. Then he confronted again what a Rene standard meant.

The four satellites were bound by one rule. Every component had been designed to respond in the same way. That was the strength of the Rene. If one failed, the rest still moved by the same value. It also meant that no single one could be given a different value.

> **Las:** “If I break the standard, the penetrators will explode.”

He could enter only one value, the same value for all four.

Las sat before the alignment axis.

> **Las:** “Erase this value, and the calculation becomes more accurate.”

> **Ara:** “More accurately destroy what?”

> **Las:** “Maldek.”

> **Ara:** “No. The gate you lost.”

Las did not answer.

He did not erase the 0.4 degrees. He entered them.

The four penetrators aimed at Maldek's center shifted 0.4 degrees in the same direction. The defense lattice divided paths of arrival. But the 0.4 degrees Las entered did not divide. They gave the same direction to all four weapons.

The launch was silent. The penetrators had no propulsion flame. There were only mass and velocity. Four dark objects descended toward the planet's night side, drawing four shadows across the dust of orbit. The lines were parallel. Still parallel, they slowly drew apart.

The first penetrator grazed the edge of Maldek. The upper atmosphere split along its path.

The second penetrator pierced the crust. Nothing erupted from the opening. It collapsed inward.

The third struck a defense satellite operated by the Rene. Its orbit changed. The satellite was flung toward Earth. It would one day become the Moon.

The fourth penetrator passed through the center of Maldek.

## VII. The Fold

The planet did not explode.

First, every sound disappeared.

Then the continents folded.

The seas rose upward, and mountain ranges were severed at the roots. Pyramid-shaped resonance towers lost their light and collapsed inward. The dome cities shifted 0.4 degrees from where they had stood. Then they vanished.

A blue fissure opened at Maldek's center.

There were no stars beyond it. There was only a black space in which nothing existed.

The Chiwoo lattice of minds was pulled inside.

Ara held the ring until the last moment. If she let go, direction would disappear. If direction disappeared, nothing would remain.

She chose one thing she could leave behind.

Not Maldek. Not Mars. A third coordinate where no one had yet arrived.

Ara placed that coordinate on Earth. She left it not as a gate but as a place. A few unshaped stones and two palm-sized minerals were enough. One fixed the position. The other remembered the difference when that position changed.

> **Ara:** “It is not too late here.”

> **Las:** “For what?”

> **Ara:** “What has not yet arrived.”

In orbit, Las looked at his instrument.

There was nothing where Maldek had been. There was no mass signal. The screen had returned to its initial state. The Martian War Council ordered him to record a victory.

Las did not record a victory.

On the last line of the measurement log, he entered a single value. He did not say what it measured.

**0.4 degrees.**

That value remained, in the same direction, in every Rene structure later raised on Earth. The builders had not been wrong. The sky they used as their reference had moved 0.4 degrees that day, and no one had written it in the record.
